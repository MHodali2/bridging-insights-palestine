Overview

Towards an evidence-based conflict resolution framework for a just and agreed upon solution to the Palestinian issue

Bridging Insights Palestine (BI-Pal) builds the ecosystem, network, and evidence base needed to enable a political process for the Palestinian issue grounded in dignity, freedom, equality, justice, and self-determination.

About the team
What we are achieving

Building the ecosystem for a just, agreed-upon solution

BI-Pal takes the first critical steps toward a political process for the Palestinian issue — built on a shared evidence base, a broad network, and a common set of values.

Building the ecosystem

BI-Pal seeks to take the first critical steps in building the ecosystem that addresses the Palestinian context.

Mobilizing diverse leaders

BI-Pal seeks to identify, engage and mobilize a significant network of diverse leaders.

Enabling a political process

BI-Pal aims to contribute to the enabling of a political process towards a solution based on core values of dignity, freedom, equality, justice and self-determination.

Solving real challenges

BI-Pal aims to find solutions to problems and challenges related to the context.

Strategic pillars

A theory-and-practice methodology

Adopting a "theory and practice" methodology, BI-Pal works through two interdependent strategic pillars.

PILLAR 01

The Evidence-Based Research Program

Learning the insights of a comparative analysis study (Why It Worked) of areas of conflict, devised and funded by Bridging Insights, Inc. and designed and led by Harvard Kennedy School's Negotiation and Conflict Resolution Collaboratory, to understand the factors that helped parties in conflict areas engage in a political process — then applying a contextualized toolkit based on that evidence to diagnose gaps and challenges and allow for interventions.

PILLAR 02

The Engagement, Outreach and Collaboration Program

Engaging the Palestinian community through both top-down and bottom-up methods, creating safe spaces where pluralism is harnessed for constructive change, and including the perspectives and insights of relevant partners and stakeholders.

Our approach

Five steps, grounded in evidence

Each step builds on the last — from studying what has worked elsewhere to creating space for change at home.

01

Learning the insights of a comparative analysis study (Why It Worked) of areas of conflict to understand the factors that helped parties in conflict areas engage in a political process.

02

Applying a contextualized toolkit for conflict resolution that is based on evidence and insights from the comparative analysis study to diagnose gaps and challenges and allow for interventions.

03

Engaging the Palestinian community through both top-down and bottom-up methods.

04

Creating safe spaces where pluralism is harnessed for constructive change.

05

Including the perspectives and insights of relevant partners and stakeholders.

Why this matters

Why this matters

01

New approaches, locally owned

There is a need for new approaches to conflict resolution that is driven and owned by relevant stakeholders.

02

Top-down and bottom-up

The dual approach ensures the inclusion of perspectives from all societal levels, fosters meaningful dialogue and cultivates a sense of agency and ownership.

03

Stronger foundations

These efforts build the foundations for equitable socio-political representation, intergenerational and cross-geographical cohesion, and stronger regional and international partnerships.

04

A reason for hope

International conflicts that were protracted and seemed to have reached a stalemate were resolved — and that gives hope to the Palestinians.

05

A more cohesive society

Research shows that a sustainable political process requires a society that is more cohesive, more resilient, and better able to organize, manage internal tensions, engage differences constructively, and respond collectively when political openings emerge.

06

Addressing internal challenges

Building on insights from studying successful political processes of areas where conflicts were resolved, it is evident that internal challenges within Palestine need to be addressed.

"Creating hope." BI-Pal, June 2026
What we do

Turning insight into action

BI-Pal works through its two pillars to generate knowledge and identify and engage the relevant stakeholders.

In practice, that means focusing on advancing young leaders, strengthening internal cohesion, endorsing a culture of dialogue, acceptance of the other, rebuilding trust, protecting civic space, and creating inclusive forms of community participation.

Who we work with

A broad, cross-sector network

Through a top-down and bottom-up approach, BI-Pal engages with academics, practitioners, civil society organizations, academic institutions, research centers, and youth networks.
